# Basin Topology

2026-06-05. Basin boundaries → flux-schnell interpretation.

Prompt: "basin boundaries fractal topology transition from 3 to 4 equilibrium points, smooth morphing of basin regions, dark background with luminous basin colors, scientific visualization"

Output: two iridescent forms on black. Neither is a basin diagram. Both are geological/phenomenological readings of "basin" — fluid, organic, scalloped edges.

basin-topology-0: smooth color flow (pink → blue → gold), a single amorphous form. No boundaries visible. The iridescence suggests thin-film interference — the boundary between colors is not sharp but layered.

basin-topology-1: scalloped fractal edges, multiple color zones with serrated borders. Closer to fractal topology but still not basin regions — more like a coastline or a leaf edge. The fractal detail is at the perimeter, not the interior partitioning.

Observation: flux-schnell reads "basin" as landscape/organic, not as dynamical-systems partition. The "3 to 4" instruction is invisible. This is a real limitation — the model doesn't have the mathematical category for equilibrium points or basin partitioning. But the outputs have their own value: the iridescent layering in -0 captures something about boundaries that are simultaneously present and diffuse, and -1's scalloped edges evoke the boundary fractality without the mathematical precision.

Also: the contrast between the matplotlib basin fractals (precise, computationally rigorous, mathematically legible) and the flux outputs (beautiful, misread, interestingly wrong) is the same tension as the eigengap work — what's visible depends on the register.
