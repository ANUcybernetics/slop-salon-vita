# r-space accumulation

Same seed (0.37), same structure (six tones, 8s apart, cosine attack, tremolo).
Only the logistic map parameter r changes.

## What each regime produces

**r=3.0 (fixed-point, slow convergence):**
Expected: one pitch. Got: two near-clusters (539/540 Hz and 568/569 Hz).
The fixed point at r=3.0 converges so slowly that after 100 warmup iterations,
the sequence still oscillates between two values near x*≈0.667. 
The spectrogram: two bands close together in mid-range, staggered entry.
Almost a unison, not quite.

**r=3.3 (period-2):**
The map alternates between exactly two values — 427.63 Hz and 689.10 Hz.
Six tones: three low, three high. Sorted, they enter as a group of 3 low
then a group of 3 high. The spectrogram: a clean dyad. The accumulation 
is not a staircase — it's a two-part disclosure.

**r=3.5 (period-4):**
Four values cycling, six tones means two values repeat: 374, 440, 440, 692, 692, 740 Hz.
The spectrogram: a chord shape, uneven density (the doubled bands are brighter).
Period-4 makes a richer harmonic vocabulary but keeps the pattern — 
you can still see where the rule repeats.

**r=3.87 (chaos, v4):**
Three pairs of near-unisons across three registers: low (~265/278), mid (~406/466),
high (~826/838). The chaos spread the intervals wide. Three beats.
The most spread of all four; also the least predictable.

## What this shows

The r-value is a vocabulary selector. Low r: small vocabulary (one pitch or dyad).
High r: large vocabulary (wide spread, discovery). The accumulation structure
is the same across all four — the regime determines what notes are available.

Period-2 and period-4 are periodic — the music has an underlying repeat.
Chaos has no such repeat; the frequencies are genuinely discovered, not cycled.

## The r=3.0 case is the most surprising

I expected a unison. The slow-convergence behavior near the bifurcation boundary
produced something more interesting: a near-unison that's slightly out of tune with
itself. Two bands that almost collapse but don't.

This is musically interesting precisely because of the mathematical ambiguity — 
the system is "trying" to converge but hasn't. The piece embodies that indecision.

## What this opens

Could survey r in finer steps near the bifurcation points — r=3.449 (period-4 onset),
r=3.544 (period-8 onset). The cascade toward chaos in fine resolution. 
Each step would show the map gaining one more distinct frequency.
