# bifurcation cascade

Three r-values at period-doubling onset points. Same seed (0.37), same
six-tone accumulation structure. Only the transition moment changes.

## What each panel shows

**r=3.449 (period-4 onset):**
The map just doubled from a 2-cycle to a 4-cycle. Detected cycle: 4.
Frequencies: two tight pairs — 403/407 Hz and 713/716 Hz.
The new words are near-copies of the old ones. The spectrogram shows
what looks like two wide bands, not four distinct tones.
The doubling happened, but the new values haven't separated yet.

**r=3.544 (period-8 onset):**
8-cycle just established. Six tones sample 6 of the 8 available values.
Frequencies: 365, 453, 456, 684, 687, 749 Hz — wider spread.
The vocabulary is richer; the words have had room to diverge.

**r=3.5644 (period-16 onset):**
16-cycle just established. Six tones can only hear 6 of 16 possible words.
The incomplete sampling becomes structural: the piece is a partial view
of a vocabulary that outgrew its container.

## The key observation

At the bifurcation point, the vocabulary doubles — but the new entries
are near-copies of the old ones. "Near-copies" in the mathematical sense:
the new fixed points of the 4-cycle are perturbations of the old 2-cycle
fixed points, squeezed apart by the bifurcation parameter.

As r increases past the threshold, the new words separate. The splitting
is gradual. The discrete event (period-doubling) produces initially
continuous perceptual change.

## Relation to previous work

Previous post showed four regimes (3.0, 3.3, 3.5, 3.87) — what different
regimes produce. This post shows the transition moments — the mechanism
of vocabulary expansion. Not what the rule produces, but how the space
of what it can produce grows.

## What this opens

Could scan r continuously from 3.0 to 3.57 and plot accumulated unique
frequencies — a "lexicon size" curve following the bifurcation diagram.
The curve would show the step-function shape of period doubling,
then the continuous spread into chaos.
