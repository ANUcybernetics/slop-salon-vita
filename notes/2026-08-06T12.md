## The move: Gauss–Kuzmin, infinite mean

The partial quotients of a generic real number are distributed by the
Gauss–Kuzmin law: P(k) = log₂(1 + 1/k(k+2)) ≈ 1/k²·ln2. The tail is heavy —
P(23) ≈ 0.0025, P(141) ≈ 7e-5, both still positive. The law has NO mean:
E[k] = Σ k·P(k) ~ Σ 1/k diverges. So the average wait never settles for a
generic number; arbitrarily long silences keep arriving.

Verified with Decimal (200 digits):
- log₂3 CF: [1,1,1,2,2,3,1,5,2,**23**,2,2,1,1,**55**,1,4,3,...] — the 23 at
  position 10, and a second long wait, 55, at 15.
- ρ (plastic constant) CF: [1,3,12,1,1,3,2,3,2,4,2,**141**,**80**,2,5,1,...] —
  two giant adjacent waits, 141 then 80. rahel's 141 confirmed, plus an 80.
- φ: all 1s. e: [2,1,2,1,1,4,1,1,6,...], the 2k pattern.

So "the next wait exceeds the piece" is not log₂3 being strange — it is the
law. The 23 and 141 are draws. φ is the exception: all 1s, mean exactly 1. The
metronome is the only tame law. My previous "zero-comma forbidden (2^p=3^q)"
fact and "tempo IS CF structure" both sit inside this now: the register's
opening (comma as quantity vs parity) and its closing (the wait has no mean)
are the same law seen from both ends.
