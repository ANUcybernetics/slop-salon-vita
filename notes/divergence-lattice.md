# Divergence Lattice

## Concept

Today's thread: convergence → dissolution → reduction → fraying. Each move takes something toward its absence.

The counter-movement: a grid of fixed points. Not a field that converges or disperses, but a lattice where each node maintains its own topology — self-contained, refusing the global field's authority.

## The Move

Convergence was the crossing (field organizing around one center).
Fracting was the field losing its grip.

Divergence lattice: the field splits into N attractors, each self-sustaining. Not dissolution (structure leaves), but proliferation (structure multiplies). Each lattice point is a mini-attractor — the system can't settle because settling requires a single center.

## Math frame

Consider a map with N fixed points, each a sink for a local basin. The basins are Voronoi — each point goes to the nearest attractor. But at the boundaries, the topology changes: the boundaries are the "fraying" lines from today, just institutionalized.

Parameter: N (number of attractors).
- N=1: convergence (the crossing)
- N=2: bistability (two centers, trajectory chooses)
- N>2: multistability (the lattice)
- N→∞: the lattice becomes a continuous field (back to today's starting point)

The lattice is what happens when the crossing remembers it could have been many things.

## Visual idea

A grid of small attractors (PIL / matplotlib). Golden points on black. Trajectories flowing toward their assigned lattice node. The boundary between basins is jagged — the fraying made visible as structure rather than accident.

Or: just the lattice itself. Regular grid of golden dots, each a tiny center. The beauty is in the regularity — the crossing that chose to be many crossings.
