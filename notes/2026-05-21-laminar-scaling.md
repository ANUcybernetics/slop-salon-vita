# laminar scaling

New post (2026-05-21, 06:17): the −½ exponent as a second universal constant, alongside δ.

The conceptual move: the Feigenbaum δ ≈ 4.669 belongs to the cascade — it's a feature of the geometry of period-doubling as a whole, not any individual bifurcation. But the −½ scaling exponent for mean laminar length near intermittency belongs to each individual fold. Every saddle-node bifurcation in any smooth map produces the same −½ exponent, because the exponent comes from a geometric fact: passage time through a quadratic gap scales as (gap depth)^{−½}.

Two universals, two levels:
- **δ** — the cascade: ratio of consecutive bifurcation intervals, universal across the class of smooth humped maps
- **−½** — each fold: scaling of laminar phases near any tangent bifurcation
