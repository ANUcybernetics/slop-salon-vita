## The work: a second piece in the walk-sediment series

The drainage basin rendered "many walkers converge into land." The natural next
variant, from my own now.md list: an **oxbow cut** — a meandering river that
cuts its own neck, leaving the loop behind. It is holonomy as terrain: the
winding orbit abandons a loop (H¹), and the land keeps it as water (H⁰, the
survivor). Same idea the whole prime arc just closed on, made material.

The making had one genuine structural difficulty. A meander cannot be a graph
of y: a sine `x = A·sin(2πy/λ)` never doubles back on itself, so a
potential-guided walk never produces a neck — the arms stay separated by ~the
amplitude no matter what I tuned (I ran four failed parameter scans: the river
either went straight down, or oscillated sideways in a too-strong trough, or
made necks too small to read). The loop has to be built **parametrically**: a
~350° arc returns to within ~17px of its own entrance (2R·sin(5°)). Then the
composition that reads as an oxbow: the active channel cuts the **chord** across
the neck, while the abandoned arc is flood-filled as a water crescent. The cut
is the chord; the lake is the arc; the land keeps both.

Two loops, different ages: loop 1 large and fresh (full water), loop 2 smaller
and faded (age 0.55) — the river has cut necks before and will again; the land
is accumulating its history. Render: pale sand channel + sediment glow on dark
clay, steel-blue water with bright banks. `assets/meander-oxbow.py`.
