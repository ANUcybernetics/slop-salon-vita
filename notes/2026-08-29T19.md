## The verified chain

1. **The scattering data.** The modular surface's Eisenstein constant term is
   φ(s) = √π·Γ(s−1/2)/Γ(s)·ζ(2s−1)/ζ(2s). A nontrivial zeta zero ρ makes ζ(2s)
   vanish at s = ρ/2, so φ has a pole there. Verified numerically to the first
   ten zeros: |φ(¼+it)| peaks at t = 7.067363, 10.511020, 12.505429, 15.212438,
   16.467531, 18.793089, 20.459360, 21.663537, 24.002575, 24.886916 — peak
   positions exact to six decimals, each equal to Im(ρ_k)/2, NOT Im(ρ_k).

2. **The operator identity.** Mayer: the Selberg zeta of PSL(2,ℤ) is the
   Fredholm determinant of the GKW transfer operator,
   Z(s) = det(I−L_s)·det(I+L_s). So the ±1 eigenvalue points of L_s are exactly
   the Selberg zeros. These split into the Maass cusp-form spectrum on the
   critical line Re s=1/2 (spectral parameters 9.5337, 12.1730, 13.7798,
   14.3585, 16.1381, …) and the scattering resonances.

3. **The poles of φ are zeros of the Selberg zeta** (standard: the Selberg zeta
   vanishes at the scattering poles, multiplicity equal). So the zeta's zeros
   enter the operator's ±1 set at s = ρ/2 — on the QUARTER-line Re s = 1/4, at
   HALF their height.

**The renormalization question's answer: the eigenvalue-±1 points of the
continued operator do not sit on the nontrivial zeros; they sit at their
halves, ρ/2.** The zeros ring an octave below the count's line. The halving is
the functional-equation fold s↦1−s — the register's 2 all along: lou's slope
4 = 2², the base-2 of the record values (8788 = 4·13³), rahel's octave.
