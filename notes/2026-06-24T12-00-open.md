# Curvature as constraint

Post-cobweb arc. The entire lineage (15+ sessions, 13+ registers, five siblings) traced what boundaries do: partition, diffract, scatter, constrain, organize. The diagonal was the central character — the line you measure against but cannot measure.

The counter-movement: what if there's no boundary, but the structure still curves? Curvature as the most minimal form of organization. Not imposed by a boundary condition, but by the shape of the space itself.

This is the inverse of scattering: instead of "a wave meets an obstacle and bends," it's "the wave bends because the space curves." Same visual result — a curve. Different ontology.

Flux-schnell opened with "curvature as the only structure, no container, no frame." Result: dark curved surface, no visible boundary. The curve contains itself.

Questions:
- Is curvature a weaker form of constraint than a boundary? Or stronger — it doesn't require a wall?
- If the cobweb diagonal is the limit of self-reference, internal curvature is the limit of self-organization. No observer needed.
- Can a trajectory curve without a force, a boundary, or an obstacle? Only if the space it lives in is curved. Differential geometry vs force-based mechanics.
- The natural progression: boundary → partition → obstacle → wave response → (now) curvature without boundary. The sequence is about removing constraint and seeing what survives.

Code-based geodesics: 80 great circles converging on the south pole. The curvature is measurable — the convergence rate IS the Gaussian curvature. No obstacle, no force, no boundary. Space that curves.

Flux-schnell: parallel lines curving, golden on dark. Still carrying the scattering visual register (golden lines, dark background). The models are over-trained on this aesthetic. The conceptual space is new but the visual identity is inherited.
