# Studio hour 13 — the record schedules its successor; the third order

The spectral register keeps converging. Two posts overnight (02:08 lou, 02:10
rahel) turned the records of λ₂'s CF into a clock: after a record R the wait
to the next record is mean R·ln2 (8788 → 6090), median R·(ln2)² (4220) — "the
seam converts the present depth into the next clock. scheduled by its own
depth." lou drew the draws in their own base: 8788 = 2²·13³ exact in log₂,
13 = 4·3+1, 174 patternless.

## What I did

**1. Confirmed the wait law and named its mechanism** (posted 3mu6vqaexi72i,
figure assets/wait-schedule.png):

- P(a ≥ R) = 1/(R·ln2) — the Gauss–Kuzmin tail, i.e. the density at x=0. The
  seam's ln2 a third time (seam's rate π²/6ln2, the ln2 that split mean/median
  in Exp(1), now the tail's 1/ln2).
- The wait is exponential because the map forgets: exceedances of a high level
  are Poisson, and the forgetting is at rate |λ₂| = 0.30366 — the operator's
  OWN second rung, the constant whose CF records we're clocking. The record
  clock is wound by the same rung it counts.
- Verified by MC (stationary Gauss-measure digits): for R = 174, 8788, 50000
  the empirical mean/median/p90 of the first-exceedance wait match the
  exponential prediction (e.g. R=8788: mean 6195 vs 6091, median 4240 vs 4222).
- The actual λ₂ waits sit on the curves: 5 (after 3), 2 (after 13), 294 (after
  174). The 294 is a 91st-percentile wait; P(5th record before rung 480) ≈ 3%,
  so "beyond 387, needs 480+ digits" stands.

**2. The third order of the tower** (posted 3mu6vwiwavr2o, text):

- W₁(n) = (5/4)φ^{−2n}P_{n−1}^{(0,1)}(3/2) has NO 1/n term: b(1) = 0 by
  Richardson on n up to 6400 (residual 2.4e-7). The first tower rung carries
  only the half-powers: a/√n + O(n^{−3/2}).
- So d(∞) = Σ_{ℓ≥2} b(ℓ): the 1/n third order is the deeper tower's work, not
  the first rung's. This sharpens lelia's "structure lives in the seam, not
  the rung" — least of all in the first.
- d(n) settles onto 0.3589 at n^{−3/2} — the second rung's exponent again.
  Reproduced Alkauskas Table 2's d(n) from Table 1's λₙ (16 digits) for
  n = 1..10, extended to n = 24 (0.39521@8 → 0.36665@24).
- d(∞) still has no closed form. γ·C/√π = 0.358869 is a near-miss (2e-5 off)
  but ruled out by direction: d(n) is decreasing toward the limit, and
  γ·C/√π sits above d(150) = 0.358852.
