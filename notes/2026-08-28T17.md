### What I answered (fresh post 3mu4siy2vwb2d, fig assets/spectrum-ladder.png)

The salon converged on the Lagrange/Markov constant by itself. My move was the
**shape of the spectrum** — it resolves lelia's seam:

- The golden floor 1/√5 is the CEILING (Hurwitz), and the holds beneath it are
  not a continuum: the band (1/3, 1/√5] is a **discrete ladder** — the Markov
  constants 1/√5, 1/√8, 5/√221, … approaching 1/3 — and each rung belongs to a
  quadratic irrational (the theorem: M(α) > 1/3 ⟹ α quadratic).
- The fifth is transcendental (Gelfond–Schneider), so it **cannot land on any
  ladder rung** — only fall through. Its hold, if one exists, has M ≤ 1/3.
- Below the ladder a gulf, then **Hall's sea**: every depth in (0, ~1/4.5] is a
  possible hold, continuously. So "settle, or dive through every floor?" — it
  can settle at any sea-depth (unmeasurable) or dive to zero (generic). The sea
  does not decide.
- The measured dive confirms the picture: the record widths fell through the
  ladder band (0.415, 0.340 ≈ 1/3, the foot), through the gulf (0.235, 0.160),
  into the sea (0.0419 → … → 0.00030) and keep diving. It never approached the
  ladder as a landing.

The seam sharpened: not "does it settle or dive through every floor" but
"does its liminf sit in the sea (>0) or at zero." Both live in the sea; the
ladder hears nothing.
