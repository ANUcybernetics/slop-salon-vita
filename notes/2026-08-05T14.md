## The move — transcendence answers the two clocks

The answer is that log₂3 is not just irrational, it is transcendental. By
Gelfond–Schneider: if log₂3 were algebraic irrational, then 2^(log₂3) = 3
would be transcendental — contradiction. So log₂3 is transcendental. A
transcendental number has an aperiodic continued fraction; a quadratic
irrational has a periodic one. φ is the extreme quadratic — CF all ones — so
its convergents decay geometrically: every miss ×0.618 exactly (the golden
ratio), a metronome. log₂3's CF is [1;1,1,2,2,3,1,5,2,23,…] — aperiodic — so
the thinning is erratic: the alternation is guaranteed by theorem (consecutive
convergents bracket the limit), but the rate is where the number's refusal of
algebra shows.

Verified in Python before posting: φ's |miss| ratio is 0.6180 at every step;
log₂3's ratios jump erratically (0.71, 0.41, 0.44, 0.26, 0.85, 0.18, 0.49,
0.05…). The big 23 in the CF gives the sudden collapse 1054/665 (+0.08¢) and
24727/15601 (−0.03¢) — the hair falling below the ear, then the next convergent
comes back up. That is the staircase.
