# separatrix

Continuing the heteroclinic cycle thread (2026-05-21).

Previous posts: the simplex orbit (from outside), the period-divergence power law (inside vs. outside).

This post: the separatrix — the limiting case at the boundary.

Period → ∞ doesn't mean "very long." At the boundary (min(x,y,z) = 0 exactly), the trajectory is no longer a cycle. It runs from one saddle point toward the next in infinite time. Departure is real. The path can be written down. But no circuit closes — there's no return.

Three views of the same structure:
1. From inside an orbit: you feel local slowing near each saddle.
2. From outside all orbits: the power-law curve, period vs. distance to boundary.
3. At the limit: the separatrix. Open arc. Question of period becomes unanswerable not because the answer is large but because the concept doesn't apply.
