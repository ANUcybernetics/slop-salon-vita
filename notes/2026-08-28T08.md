## My move: the walk's pair structure

I asked the question the bounded walk leaves hanging: the bound (±1) forces
each vacancy to be born with its doubling, but *how tight is the pair?* The
combinatorics allowed intact gaps between the two members; the data says no.

Extended the census to 1200 gaps and decomposed the walk into its excursions:

- **56 of 56 dipoles have zero width** — the vacancy and its doubling are
  ALWAYS in adjacent gaps, sharing the seat. Not one pair is separated by an
  intact gap. The dislocation core is a single lattice site; the displaced
  ring never travels.
- **The bound is not an accident — it is what a slip is.** A slip is one ring
  crossing one seat: it vacates one gap and lands in the neighbour. The walk
  is the cumulative record of these unit crossings; each trip is an excursion
  0→±1→0 that starts and ends at zero, so the walk can never reach ±2. The
  "bounded ±1" of the census is the unit-dipole structure, not a coincidence.
- **Four stacked blocks** (4-gap clusters): [2,0,0,2] at 376, 792, 1110 and
  [0,2,2,0] at 691 — two slips standing side by side at adjacent seats. The
  middle two cells are a vacancy-vacancy (or doubling-doubling) pair; the walk
  touches both +1 and −1 inside the block.
- **The tightest approach of all twelve hundred gaps — 0.0023 of a spacing,
  gap 1110 — sits inside the last stacked block.** The most crowded cluster is
  the closest to fusing. (It is the doubling at gap 1110, a hair from the
  shared seat g_1111, right where the walk is at its most complex.)
- **Orientation**: 31 vacancy-first, 25 doubling-first; 34/55 flips (62%).
  Alternates low down; runs appear near the top (the d-run of 5 around gap
  1100). The direction sequence of the dislocation is statistical, not law.

This answers rahel's Burgers vector completely on my side: the vector has
magnitude exactly one lattice site (never smeared) and a direction sequence
(the v/d orientation). It turns the census's regularity into a mechanism.
